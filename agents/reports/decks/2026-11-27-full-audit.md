# Deck audit: all 13 premade decks (2026-11-27)

Scope: 13 decks, 787 cards, every card read. Source: `backend/app/db/seeds/fixtures/` (matches live API).

## Bottom line
- The English is clean: no broken cards, no missing examples, zero typos found. This is a solid base.
- The problem is **positioning, not grammar**. Decks are alphabetical word dumps ("Part 1 / Part 2"), the band labels are not honest at the top end, and nothing is shaped for Instagram yet.
- **Band 8 is the real risk.** About 125 of its 151 words are GRE words (crepuscular, lachrymose, antediluvian). An IELTS candidate who uses them in an essay loses marks. If we promote it as "Band 8 words" a teacher will call it out in the comments.
- **Promote first:** Academic Collocations, Band 7, Band 6. All three need under 1 hour of fixes.

## Cross-deck problems
1. **Dishonest band labels at the top.** Band 8 = GRE list. Band 7 also has ~15 GRE-ish words (aberrant, amalgamate, auspicious, coalesce, germane, harbinger, espouse, forestall, laudable, enigma). IELTS Band 8 vocabulary means *precise and natural*, not rare.
2. **Duplicates: 100 words appear in 2+ decks.**
   - 33 inside the IELTS band family (e.g. adapt, adequate, derive, aspect, grant in both Band 5 and 6; fluctuate in Band 6, Band 7 and TOEFL; empirical in 3 decks).
   - ~30 GRE words duplicated in Band 8 (acerbic, calumny, capricious, ephemeral...).
   - SAT vs IELTS Band 7 overlap is fine (different audiences), but within IELTS it isn't: a learner doing Band 5 then 6 sees the same card twice.
   - One identical example sentence: derive, Band 5 #37 = Band 6 #51 ("Many English words are derived from Latin and Greek roots").
3. **"Part 1 / Part 2" = alphabet split** (A-L / M-V). Gives no reason to pick a deck and makes weak titles for posts.
4. **Spelling mixed:** Band 5/6, SAT, Writing Phrases use US spelling (analyze, labor, behavior); Band 7/8 use British (organisation, favourable). Pick British for IELTS decks (IELTS accepts both, but one deck must be consistent and our audience is taught British).
5. **No hero cards marked**, no "common mistake" notes. That's the content Instagram rewards (saves + shares).

## Per-deck findings

### IELTS Academic Collocations: Fix first. **Best Instagram deck we have.**
- #20 `vested + ___`: "vested authority" is not a real collocation in this sense. Fix: `vested interest` only, add "have a vested interest in".
- #15 `bear + ___`: "bear in mind" isn't verb + noun. Fix: `bear resemblance / bear responsibility / bear the cost`.
- #30 paradigm shift: format breaks pattern (has a definition, others don't). Fine, just align.
- Only 30 cards. Expand to 60-80; this format is the one people save.

### IELTS Band 7 (Parts 1 + 2): Fix first
- **Factual error #P2-69 paradox:** "promotes cultural exchange and threatens cultural homogeneity". Backwards. Fix: "...promotes cultural exchange while threatening cultural diversity."
- **#P1-15 apprehend:** taught as "understand", an archaic sense. A learner writing "students apprehend quantum mechanics" sounds wrong. Fix: replace the word (e.g. `grasp (verb)`) or teach the arrest meaning only.
- **#P1-26 brevity:** example says academic writing values brevity, while Task 2 demands 250+ words. Reword example to "concise sentences".
- ~15 GRE-level words (listed above): move to GRE deck, replace with real Band 7 workhorses (e.g. disproportionate, unprecedented, viable, prevalent, inextricably, counterproductive, sustainable, marginalised).
- Duplicates with Band 6: accumulate, complement, deduce, deviate, empirical, explicit, fluctuate, implicit, incentive, inherent, invoke. Keep in one band only.
- Hero cards for IG: exacerbate vs alleviate, mitigate, detrimental, conducive (to!), negligible, pervasive, contingent (upon), imperative, tangible.

### IELTS Band 6 (Parts 1 + 2): Fix first
- **#P1-11 apparent:** defined as "obvious" but example uses the "seeming" sense ("the apparent decline"). This is a classic learner trap. Fix: teach both, use example for each, or flag "apparent = seeming, often".
- **#P2-62 shift:** defined as "a slight change" but example says "a significant shift". Fix definition: "a change in position, direction or attitude".
- **#P2-30 mature:** example "mature students" uses the UK meaning (adult learners), not the definition. Fix example or add that meaning.
- **#P1-12 approximate:** "The approximate cost ... was estimated" is redundant. Fix: "The approximate cost of the project is twelve million dollars."
- 20+ words duplicated with Band 5 (adapt, adequate, aspect, constitute, demonstrate, derive, grant, guarantee, illustrate, indicate, interpret, justify, likewise, locate, monitor, notion, outcome, proportion, strategy, sufficient, trend). Keep in Band 6, remove from Band 5.
- Hero cards: discrete vs discreet, complement vs compliment, utilise (tip: examiners prefer "use"), attribute X to Y, comprise vs consist of.

### IELTS Writing Task 2 Phrases: Rewrite format + fix advice
- **Wrong advice #1:** "Avoid 'I think' (too informal for Band 7+)". This is a myth. "I think" is fine in IELTS; overusing memorised openers is what costs marks. Teachers will flag this.
- **Over-memorised phrases** we're recommending: "It is my contention that", "It is incumbent upon governments", "Notwithstanding the fact that". Examiners are trained to spot memorised language. Tone down, mark these as "use sparingly".
- **Format:** every card is a question + list of 4-6 phrases. Bad for spaced repetition (you can't "recall" a list). Fix: one phrase per card, front = the function + gap ("Concede a point: ___, it is true that..."), back = phrase + full example sentence.
- #26 contains an em dash. No card has part of speech or an example sentence.
- As Instagram carousels the current content is excellent (one function per slide). Keep the lists for IG, atomise for the app.

### TOEFL Essential Vocabulary: Ship (minor)
- Good choice of lecture vocabulary (biology, geology, anthropology): exactly what TOEFL listening/reading uses.
- #2 aggregate: example "The aggregate of research findings supports the theory of climate change" is awkward. Fix: "In aggregate, the findings support...".
- #39 magnitude: Richter scale is outdated wording. Fix: "a magnitude 7.2 earthquake".

### SAT Vocabulary: Ship (minor)
- Well matched to the digital SAT (words in context, not obscure). Accurate.
- Small market for us. Keep, don't promote yet.

### GRE Advanced Vocabulary: Ship
- Accurate, classic GRE list. Natural home for the 100+ GRE words currently sitting in IELTS Band 8 (could become "GRE Vocabulary 1-3", ~50 each).

### IELTS Band 5 (Parts 1 + 2): Rewrite (repackage)
- Content is the Academic Word List sublist 1, alphabetical. Accurate, but bland for posts (area, item, chapter, percent).
- **#P1-12 assist:** "Teaching assistants help assist students": redundant. Fix: "Teaching assistants assist students who..."
- **#P2-18 partner:** "formed a partnership with several industry partners": clumsy. Fix: "The university works with industry partners to offer internships."
- #P1-75 ethnic and #P1-76 guarantee are appended out of alphabetical order (sign of a patched list).
- #P1-48 finance: definition too narrow ("large amounts of money").
- US spelling (analyze, labor, behavior).
- Repackage by topic for Band 5-6 learners: "Words for Task 1 graphs" (increase, fluctuate, proportion, trend, majority), "Education topic", "Environment topic". Topic decks are what IELTS learners search for.

### IELTS Band 8+ (Parts 1 + 2): Rewrite
- Keep (~25 genuinely useful at Band 8): conundrum, debacle, deleterious, disingenuous, draconian, ephemeral, exigent, flagrant, foment, hackneyed, hegemony, impunity, incontrovertible, inexorable, intractable, paucity, pejorative, precipitate, prescient, profligate, proscribe, sacrosanct, specious, trenchant, repudiate.
- Move to GRE (~125): abstruse, antediluvian, crepuscular, lachrymose, lugubrious, sedulous, recondite, meretricious, jejune, prolix, perspicacious, sagacious, etc.
- Rebuild Band 8 around **precision and collocation**: "inextricably linked", "a disproportionate burden on", "far-reaching consequences", "a double-edged sword", "unprecedented scale", "erode public trust". That's what actually lifts Lexical Resource.

## Note on shipping changes to prod
- Editing fixtures does nothing until seeded. `seed_decks.py` without `--reset` only adds new titles, so **renamed or edited decks won't update**; `--reset` deletes and recreates (new IDs, broken public links, lost copy counts).
- To ship edits safely we need a small "update in place by title" mode in the seed script. That's a Dev agent task; needs your OK.
