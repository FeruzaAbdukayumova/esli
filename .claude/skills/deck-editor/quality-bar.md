# Deck quality bar

A deck is **Instagram-ready** only if every line below is true.

## Card
- **Front:** the word or phrase + part of speech, e.g. `Mitigate (verb)`. One item per card. No trick fronts.
- **Back:** definition in 15 words or fewer, using words simpler than the target word.
- **Example:** one sentence, 8-20 words, natural, exam-topic flavoured, shows the typical collocation (`mitigate the risk`, not just `mitigate`).
- Optional, only if it helps: common collocations, a "don't confuse with" note, register (formal/informal).
- Total back text short enough to read in 5 seconds on a phone.
- Consistent format inside a deck (same order: definition, then example).
- No em dashes, no typos, straight quotes consistent.

## Deck
- **Level is honest:** a Band 6 word is used by Band 6-7 writers, not a GRE word dropped into IELTS.
- **No duplicates** inside the deck or across the deck family (e.g. Band 7 Part 1 vs Part 2).
- **Size:** 30-80 cards. Split bigger decks by theme, not alphabet.
- **Title** says who it's for and the result: "IELTS Band 7 Words for Writing Task 2" beats "Vocabulary Part 1".
- **Tags** filled and consistent (`ielts,vocabulary,band-7,test-prep`).

## Instagram appeal
- At least 5 "hero cards": words people misuse, surprise words, or words that upgrade a sentence instantly.
- The deck answers a real search or pain: "words to get Band 7", "phrases for Task 2 intro".
- You can describe the deck's promise in one line that makes someone tap the link.

## Verdicts
- **Ship to Instagram:** passes everything.
- **Fix first:** structure good, under 25% of cards need edits.
- **Rewrite:** wrong level, weak examples or bad structure across the deck.
- **Cut:** no audience or fully duplicated elsewhere.
